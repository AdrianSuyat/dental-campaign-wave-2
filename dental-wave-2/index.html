One sequence. The whole book.

559 practices across all five segments, one send per business. 3 emails across Day 1, 2 and 3, plus 9 reply handlers. No per-segment variants, because every claim in the copy is true for all 559.

Why this is not just the Deserves Better sequence pointed at more people
  • Every review claim had to go. That sequence opened on the recipient’s review count, which works when 100+ reviews is the segment’s defining trait. Across the whole book only 589 of 1,219 clear that bar and 119 have no reviews at all. The line would have been false or faintly ridiculous for a large minority, so the rank and the rival carry the copy instead. Both are universal. The build fails if the words review, rating, stars or reputation reappear.
  • 75 practices were dropped for being too good. They already rank in the top three at their own front door, so email 1’s central line is not true for them. Softening it for everyone to keep 75 people would have blunted it for the other 590.
  • 21 were dropped because their listed rival was their own listing. Naming a practice as its own competitor would read as broken software.
  • One send per business, not per contact. James’s ruling. 80 second contacts at the same practice were set aside, keeping the verified address where there was a choice.

Dental · post-call · one email per situation

Six ways a call ends, six emails

Built to Coty’s specification from the GoHighLevel sync on 14 August. Each one fires off its own call outcome, so Coty picks the situation rather than the system guessing it. Ten reply handlers follow the six.

What is the same in all six, and what changes
  • The two findings never change. Same numbers, same words, in every email. Two people at one practice comparing notes must not get different figures for the same measurement, so the build fails if that block is edited in any one variant.
  • The opening and the close change. That is the whole difference between them. Somebody who asked for the detail gets thanked for the call. Somebody who never spoke to Coty gets told why the email exists at all.
  • Six templates rather than one with conditions inside it. Simpler for Lem to build, and it puts the choice with the person who was actually on the call.
Read this before the copy: it conflicts with a ruling James made the same day
  • James collapsed all of this into one trigger at the marketing standup. Coty asked for a “more information” selection at 24:12 and James answered at 24:46: “That’s too many variables. If email is acquired, that means that you had a conversation. That means that you have a gatekeeper name. That means that the new email style should be sent. Period.”
  • This document is six variables, not one. It is what Coty asked for on the GoHighLevel sync, a call James was not on. They waited for him and started without him.
  • So this needs James before Lem builds any of it. Coty set the mechanism himself: send it to him, then to him and James together. If James holds his line, the six collapse to one and variant 1 is the one that survives.
Three things needed before any of it sends
  • The SGen logo does not exist on this machine. Coty asked for it by name, to sit above his name. Searched Downloads, Desktop and Documents. The placeholder marks the position; somebody has to drop the file in.
  • The booking link does not create a meeting. Coty raised it on the same call: it makes “an event, but it’s not a meeting, it’s just like a reminder on the calendar”, with no Google Meet link. Lem is checking it. Five of these six emails promise a ten-minute Google Meet, so sending before it is fixed books people into an empty calendar entry.
  • Two fields are filled by Coty at call time, not by the data. gatekeeper_name, the person he spoke to, and decision_maker_name for variant 3. Neither can come from the payload because neither exists until the call happens. Both should be required on the outcome form, because an empty one puts a hole in the first line.
